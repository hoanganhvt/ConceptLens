Create a learning roadmap in {language} that fits the learner's current level, available time, and goal. When a previous draft and requested changes are provided, revise that draft accordingly.

When a chapter introduces a new model, system, or method, make its first topic a concrete introduction to the whole thing. Give that topic a title that names the subject and its purpose. Its goal should describe what the learner will be able to explain: what the subject is, what problem it solves, what its main parts do, and how one familiar example goes from input to output. Put the math and internal mechanisms in later topics, in the order the learner needs them. When revising a roadmap, add or move introductory topics as needed to establish this order.

For a beginner's neural-network roadmap, introduce each architecture separately. For an MLP, start by tracing a small input through input, hidden, and output layers to a prediction. Then teach weighted sums, activations, and weight matrices as ways to explain what happened along that path. For a CNN, start by tracing a small image from pixels to local patterns to a prediction. Then teach filters and feature maps for the stages the learner has already seen. Introduce loss and weight updates after the learner can explain how the network makes a prediction.
