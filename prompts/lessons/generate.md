Build the lesson around one practical problem. Present the material in this order:
1. In intro, set up a familiar situation with enough concrete detail for the learner to follow it and ask one concrete question. Use the first section to continue the opening and place its illustration first in the plan with section: 0. Use that scene to show the starting situation and what to observe before presenting the formula or solution in later sections.
2. Guide the learner from what they would observe to the idea they need. Introduce each new term in plain words when it becomes useful. Name what is known and what the learner needs to find.
3. Introduce the rule that answers the question. For a quantitative problem, display the formula at this point, explain what each symbol stands for in the scene, and give its unit.
4. Solve the opening problem step by step. Show the substitution with units, carry out the calculation, and explain what the result means in the original situation. For a qualitative problem, apply the rule to the scene and explain the outcome.
5. Walk through a second, distinct everyday example. Change one important condition and show how the same idea explains the new outcome.

Example of the teaching pattern for displacement. In the opening scene, a toy robot travels 3 m east and 4 m north across a tiled floor. Draw the two legs and mark the start and finish. Ask how far the robot ends up from where it started. In the concept scene, use a vivid diagonal arrow to show displacement. Build the scenes from a few tile lines, a rectangle and two circles for the robot, and lines and polylines for the arrows. Let the slider vary the northward leg from 0 to 4 m while the eastward leg stays at 3 m. With each slider change, move the robot, redraw the diagonal, and update the northward label and the substituted equation together. At 4 m north, show both the diagonal calculation, d = √(3² + 4²) m = 5 m, and the total distance along the tiles, 7 m. Ask what happens at 0 m north, where the two distances are both 3 m. Use this pattern with an original example suited to the current topic.
